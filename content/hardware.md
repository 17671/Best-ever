Introduction:
        - Computers have become a vital part of everyday life; they influence events both at an individual scale and large
    - Computers include technology such as smart-phones, cars, washing machine, microwave ovens, game consoles and many more
- Without computers, these wouldn’t exist.

I.The First Computers
        - The foundation of computers started during the 1940’s
        - The market for them was not popular 
    "I think there is a world market for maybe five computers" (Thomas Watson, Chairman of IBM, 1943)
    "There is no reason for any individual to have a computer in his home" (Ken Olsen, President of Digital Equipment Corporation, 1977)
    - A drop in the size of computers was anticipated, but never at the scales we see today.
- Their purpose was as powerful automatic calculators

II.Fundemental Parts of a Computer
        - Input devices are used to send data to the computer
    - Early versions read data from paper tape
    - Modern versions include the keyboard, bar-code scanner, switches and buttons of a microwave
        - Output devices are used to display the results 
    - Cross-temporary examples include the actuator that switches on a pump of a computer controlled central heating system or the sound system that generates the beep of an electronic heart monitor
        - Hybrid devices work both as input and output devices
- They mark a modern era, with examples such as touchscreen and the electric typewriter

III.The Programmable Computer
        - The computer program follows instructions to process input and produce the output
        - A QR-code reader sends the name of a scanned bar-coded product and it’s details to the phone. The app has been programmed with a set of instructions that makes it:
    1.Takes in data via the scanner
    2.Uses the data to look up the name of the product in a list that cross references QR-code data to product name
    3.Generates a new form of data that is compatible with showing the product name and image on display
        - Programmable refers to a set of instructions that can be changed according to the needed output
    - It requires a certain level of versatility, since the program is asked to produce carried results

IV.Computers and Data
        - Information which is presented in a very strictly controlled format is called Data
        - A computer can’t preform it’s task if the information it needs has not been transformed into the required data form

V.Computers Today
        - Cheaper and smaller, don’t resemble the early versions of computers
        - The cost of computer power keeps decreasing, making any object able to incorporate computer technologies regardless how small, cheap or disposable.
        - They are able to ‘communicate’ with each other across the World Wide Web
    - Applications that run on personal computers and mobile phones help us communicate with each other and with other computers

A quote from Danny Hills, a pioneer in the development of the fastest computers
"I went to my first computer conference at the New York Hilton about 20 years ago. When somebody there predicted the market for microprocessors [these are the major component of all computers] would eventually be in the millions, someone else said, ‘Where are they all going to go? It’s not like you need a computer in every doorknob!’ Years later, I went back to the same hotel. I noticed the room keys had been replaced by electronic cards you slide into slots in the doors.
There was a computer in every doorknob." (Danny Hills, circa 1999)
