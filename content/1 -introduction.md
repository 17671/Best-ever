# Topic 1: Computer Hardware

## Introduction

- Computers have become a vital part of everyday life, influencing events at both individual and global scales.
- Computers include technologies such as smartphones, cars, washing machines, microwave ovens, game consoles, and many more.
- Without computers, many of these technologies would not exist.

## I. The First Computers

- The development of the first modern electronic computers began during the 1940s.
- At that time, the market for computers was very limited.
  - "I think there is a world market for maybe five computers." — Thomas Watson, Chairman of IBM, 1943.
  - "There is no reason for any individual to have a computer in his home." — Ken Olsen, President of Digital Equipment Corporation, 1977.
- A reduction in the size of computers was anticipated, but not on the scale we see today.
- Early computers were primarily designed to function as powerful automatic calculators.

## II. Fundamental Parts of a Computer

### 1. Input Devices

- Input devices are used to send data to a computer.
- Early computers could read data from paper tape.
- Modern examples include:
  - Keyboards
  - Barcode scanners
  - Switches and buttons on microwave ovens

### 2. Output Devices

- Output devices are used to display or communicate the results of computer processing.
- Examples include:
  - An actuator that switches on a pump in a computer-controlled central heating system.
  - A sound system that generates a beep on an electronic heart monitor.

### 3. Hybrid Devices

- Hybrid devices function as both input and output devices.
- Examples include:
  - Touchscreens
  - Electric typewriters

## III. The Programmable Computer

- A computer program consists of instructions that process input data and produce output.

### Example: Scanning a Product

A QR-code reader or barcode scanner can send product information to a phone. An app can then use a set of programmed instructions to:

1. Take in data through the scanner.
2. Use the data to look up the product name in a list that connects codes to product information.
3. Generate output in a format suitable for displaying the product's name and image on the screen.

- **Programmable** means that a computer can follow a set of instructions that can be changed to achieve different results.
- Computers require versatility because their programs may need to produce different outputs depending on the task.

## IV. Computers and Data

- Information presented in a strictly defined format is called **data**.
- A computer cannot perform a task correctly if the information it needs has not been converted into the required data format.

## V. Computers Today

- Modern computers are cheaper and smaller and look very different from early computers.
- The cost of computing power continues to decrease, allowing computer technology to be incorporated into objects regardless of their size, cost, or expected lifespan.
- Computers can communicate with one another through networks, including the World Wide Web.
- Applications running on personal computers and mobile phones help people communicate with one another and with other computers.

## A Computer in Every Doorknob

Danny Hillis, a pioneer in the development of high-performance computers, recalled a prediction about the future market for microprocessors:

> "I went to my first computer conference at the New York Hilton about 20 years ago. When somebody there predicted the market for microprocessors [these are the major component of all computers] would eventually be in the millions, someone else said, ‘Where are they all going to go? It’s not like you need a computer in every doorknob!’ Years later, I went back to the same hotel. I noticed the room keys had been replaced by electronic cards you slide into slots in the doors.
>
> There was a computer in every doorknob."

— Danny Hillis, circa 1999
